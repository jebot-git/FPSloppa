# Cindercoil — TITANBALL

Select **TITANBALL → Cindercoil** in Host or practice. `tb_cindercoil` is an
original BSP29 industrial map. Its road wraps through 270 degrees around a
central complex and climbs 14 m from the attacker hangar to the defender bunker.
The route includes short, level approaches at both bases.

- Exactly 350 m of Titan travel, with 80 / 150 / 120 m legs.
- Checkpoints at 80/230 m; awards after the usual rear clearance at 90/240 m.
- Four connecting tunnels at 56/142/234/315 m meet in a central hall.
- Three overpasses at 92/179/248 m, each reached by switchback towers on both
  sides of the road. Decks are 13.2 m above the local route, clearing the Titan.
- Paired 4.2 m firing platforms at 286/326 m and alternating shoulder cover.
- Sheltered attacker spawn groups at 0/72/222 m and defenders at 350 m, with
  four positions per group. Spawns are 17 m off the road centre.
- Six shared dispensers: route positions 0/70/104/215/246/348 m. No natural
  pickups or proximity-based spawn resupply.
- Standard closed hangar and firing slits, 60-second preparation, fixed 10:00
  active clock and two once-only +3:00 checkpoint extensions.

Both teams can use the tunnels, towers, overpasses and dispensers. The central
hall provides shortcuts across the circular road; the Titan follows the road.
The navigation path between the 56 m and 315 m entrances is 87.7 m, compared
with 259 m along the Titan route. The 56→234 m and 142→315 m crossings take
86.5 m and 85.6 m respectively. All twelve directed connections between the
four hall entrances are verified. See the [layout plan](layout.svg); gold
passages are inside the central complex, below its roof.
The climb preserves the robot's existing horizontal chassis and collision
envelope. It does not introduce terrain IK or change movement speed.

## Source and rebuilding

`tb_cindercoil.map` and `cindercoil.wad` are editable in a Quake brush editor.
32 Quake units equal one game metre. `route.json` contains the exact game curve
and quarter-metre samples; `probes.json` supplies traversal and camera probes.
Geometry is generated by `tools/titanball/cindercoil/build.py`; regeneration
overwrites the MAP, so preserve manual edits separately.

From the project root, using Godot 4.7.2 and ericw-tools 0.18.1:

```sh
godot --headless --xr-mode off --path . --script tools/titanball/cindercoil/route.gd
python3 tools/titanball/cindercoil/build.py --compiler /path/to/ericw-tools/bin
godot --headless --xr-mode off --path . --script tools/titanball/cindercoil/prepare.gd
godot --headless --xr-mode off --path . --script tools/titanball/cindercoil/acceptance.gd
```

The builder reuses the project's local Makkon/LibreQuake texture archives. It
runs QBSP, full VIS and coloured lighting with bounce and baked dirt. `--fast-vis`
is an iteration option. `-noclip` omits unused Quake expanded clip hulls;
Godot collision uses the BSP draw geometry. The sealed draw hull, BSP29 header
and 25 MB import limit are checked. Compiler warnings are retained in the logs.

Preparation writes raw, BC7 and ASTC4 caches and texture-dictionary aliases.
`-- --raw-only` skips compression during geometry iteration. Acceptance opens
the hangar, tests the real Titan drive, bakes navigation from the compiled map,
retains the component connected to the attacker base, and checks spawn,
resupply and elevated-position reachability from both bases. Real fighters
walk every tunnel and tower in both directions. `navigation-sha256.txt` records
the BSP used for the bake. Rebuild the internal asset installer after changes.

For native Vulkan screenshots:

```sh
godot --path . --xr-mode off --rendering-method mobile --rendering-driver vulkan --script tools/titanball/cindercoil/preview.gd
```

Results and screenshots are under `test-results/cindercoil/`. This is a new
playtest map; human balance and headset performance remain unmeasured.
